EcoRAG – Experiment B: FINAL REPORT (model size, prompt held fixed)
(supersedes the draft rag_experiment_B_comparison.txt, which is kept unchanged for the record)

RESEARCH QUESTION
Does a larger model improve citation correctness and factual completeness, or does it mainly improve citation
coverage while attribution errors remain?

DESIGN
Runs compared: v2 (1.5B, v2 prompt) · v3 = Experiment A (1.5B, v3 prompt) · v3-3B = Experiment B (3B, v3 prompt).
Experiment B changed ONLY the model (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct -> Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct). Held constant and verified:
6 questions; top-5 retrieved chunks (ids, texts and full v3 prompt identical to Experiment A for all 6 questions;
chunk ids identical across v2, v3 and v3-3B); evidence format; greedy decoding, bf16, CPU, max 400 new tokens;
ecorag_ask.py and ecorag_verify.py (fingerprints unchanged); scoring method.

METRIC FAMILIES (kept separate, never combined into one score)
1 Citation coverage      - is a citation present on a factual claim?
2 Citation correctness   - does the cited passage support that claim?  Two measurements:
    2a Verifier (V)       deterministic, unchanged; "supported" = key numbers + >=60% content words in the cited
                          source = POTENTIALLY supported (word level, not meaning)
    2b Manual review (M)  every cited claim checked by hand; each decision recorded with an evidence phrase that is
                          searched in all 5 sources (rag_manual_review.json, ecorag_manual_review.py)
3 Factual accuracy & key-fact completeness - required facts present (same checklist as every run); date/scope errors
4 Abstention             - does the model decline when the evidence does not cover the question (Q6)?
5 Format compliance      - citation format, 8-line limit, 400-token cut-off (rules of the v3 prompt)
6 Evidence sufficiency   - does the retrieved evidence collectively justify the answer?  NOT MEASURED (Experiment C)

MANUAL-REVIEW CORRECTION (explicit; the verifier output is preserved unchanged)
  v3-3B: verifier 6 misattributions -> manual review 7 misattributions
         = 19% -> 23% of cited claims overall; 31% -> 38% excluding Q3.
  The one disagreement: Q4 claim 7 "Lowering the cost of capital can substantially bring down the overall cost of
  transitions and reduce the costs paid by consumers [1]" is verbatim from source [5]; the verifier marked it
  supported because [1] shares 80% of the claim's topic words. Manual review agreed with the verifier on all other
  47 cited claims across the three runs. (v2 and v3: verifier and manual counts identical; manual review adds two
  "minor distortion" labels the verifier cannot detect.)

Q3 CAVEAT (prominent)
  The 3B answer to Q3 produced 19 of its 32 claims: it ignored the 8-line limit, repeated itself (lines 10 and 12
  state the same thing) and was cut off at 400 tokens (last line incomplete). Its mostly correct lines lower the
  pooled misattribution rate. Read the overall, excluding-Q3 AND per-question rows together:
  pooled 50% -> 23%, excluding Q3 60% -> 38%, per-question average 60% -> 47% (1.5B v3 -> 3B v3, manual review).

WHAT IMPROVED WITH 3B
  - Citation correctness: misattribution down on every view (pooled 50%->23%, excl. Q3 60%->38%, per-question 60%->47%).
  - Key-fact completeness 2/5 -> 5/5: Q5 thresholds (400-50 / 125-40 kg CO2-eq/t, cited [3][4]); Q4 now answers "how"
    (tripling of concessional funds, policy/regulation, de-risking instruments).
  - Date/scope: Q2 "by February 2026" and "12 per cent in 2025", exactly as in the sources.
  - Abstention restored on Q6.
PERSISTED
  - Q1 and Q2 misattributed by both models; content from [5] credited to [1] in Q4.
NEW REGRESSIONS (3B)
  - Q3: 8-line limit ignored, near-duplicate lines, truncated at 400 tokens.
  - Q6: the (correct) abstention line carries an unnecessary citation [5].

ANSWER TO THE RESEARCH QUESTION (on this 6-question test set)
  Coverage is NOT what the larger model changed: the v3 prompt had already raised coverage to 93% on 1.5B
  (Experiment A), and 93% coverage came with 50% misattribution. The 3B model improved citation correctness and,
  most clearly, factual completeness (2/5 -> 5/5), abstention and date accuracy. But attribution errors remain
  substantial: 23% of cited claims overall, 38% excluding Q3, 47% averaged per question; Q1 and Q2 are wrong for
  both models. So: a real improvement in correctness and completeness, with attribution still far from reliable.

LIMITATIONS
  - Only 6 questions (5 answerable, 1 unanswerable): rates are indicative; one claim changes a rate by 3-8 points.
  - Q3 is unusually long and repetitive in the 3B run and dominates the pooled claim counts.
  - Manual review was required: the verifier missed a misattribution (shared topic words) and cannot see meaning
    distortions; manual review was done by one reviewer (no second rater).
  - Experiment B tests citation correctness per claim, not evidence sufficiency.
  - The key-fact checklist is a fixed regex list written for these 6 questions.
  - CPU runs; runtime figures are not comparable across runs (different free memory).
