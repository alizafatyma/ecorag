EcoRAG – Experiment A: citation-focused answer format (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, CPU)

Held constant: PDFs, chunks, BGE embeddings, Chroma, retrieval (top-5, content/table), the 6 questions,
the retrieved evidence (verified identical, chunk-for-chunk and character-for-character), evidence labels [n],
model, decoding (greedy), and the verifier (ecorag_verify.py logic unchanged; re-verifying v2 gives a
byte-identical result file).
Changed: only the answer-generation instructions (PROMPT_VERSION v2 -> v3).
v3 = one factual claim per line, each line ending with its source number(s) [n], no intro/conclusion,
max 8 lines, partial-abstention line allowed, plus the v2 rules (only sources, key facts, copy numbers).

VERIFIER TOTALS (same verifier for every run; "supported" = key numbers + >=60% content words found in
the cited source = POTENTIALLY supported, not semantically proven)

CONCLUSION
The rigid format fixes citation COVERAGE (14% -> 93%) but not citation ATTRIBUTION: half of the citations point
to the wrong source. Correctly cited claims rose from 2 to 6 (5 confirmed by hand), misattributions from 1 to 7.
Side effects: abstention broke on Q6 and Q5 lost its key thresholds; generic closing sentences disappeared.
The 1.5B model complies with "put a number on every line" but does not reliably bind that number to the
evidence it used. Next: Experiment B (Qwen2.5-3B, v3 frozen) to measure the model-size effect with the same
verifier. Caveat: 6 questions is a small test set; rates are indicative, not precise.
